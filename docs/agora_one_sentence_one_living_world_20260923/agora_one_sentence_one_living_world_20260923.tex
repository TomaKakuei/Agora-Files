\noindent\textbf{Abstract.}
Generating a social world requires inhabitants to change shared spatial,
material, and social state. We present \agora, a system that compiles a short
premise into a persistent rendered environment with roles, institutions,
possessions, and private knowledge. Humans and language agents use generated
action opportunities or propose context-specific actions, whose effects a
World Coordinator adjudicates against shared state. A masked model-comparison
survey yields ten eligible returns, forty image comparisons, and judgments
on all 21 pairs among seven language-model configurations using a shared
pipeline and premise. Preferences differ by criterion: Gemini 2.5 Flash
receives 8/10 exploration preferences, while GPT-5.6 Terra and Sol receive
11/13 and 9/11 map-readability preferences, respectively. These sparse
fixed-artifact judgments do not establish a general model ranking. An internal
generation control records higher completion for the staged procedure at
higher cost; its structure judgments are reported separately and do not
benchmark Agora against independent systems. Five two-round interaction
runs produce 53 events and 49/56 successful action results. In a retrospective
audit of 21 scripted 24-round trajectories, 1,365/1,512 actions execute
successfully, but only 398 successes change inventory or location. Together,
these findings distinguish construction reliability, artifact preference,
and enacted consequences. We release inspectable world artifacts, returned
survey analysis, and reproducible evidence; behavioral fidelity and useful
human collaboration remain unestablished.
