\section{World Construction Results}
\label{sec:construction}

\subsection{Paired Protocol and Completion}
The generation comparison uses Gemini 3 Flash Preview at temperature 0.2 and
low thinking level for both methods. Five primary premises have three
repetitions each; five held-out briefs have one each, giving 20 trials per
method. The held-out set and evaluator were frozen before held-out generation.
The requests include multi-sentence design constraints; this is not a strict
one-sentence-input test (Appendix~\ref{app:extended}). The analysis unit is the
shared brief: primary-world binary outcomes use a majority
over the three repetitions, while held-out outcomes use the single trial.

The single-pass baseline receives a 24,576-token output cap, three times the
8,192-token cap of one specialized stage. This provides a strong single-call
allowance, but does not equalize total computation across methods. The
compositional pipeline makes multiple calls and can use local retries. We
therefore report first-pass and eventual completion separately, retaining
failed outputs. The comparison evaluates the two complete generation
procedures; it does not isolate decomposition at equal total token budget.
This joint reporting of reliability and cost follows the evaluation concerns
in \citet{agentsmatter}.

Compositional generation completes 10/10 worlds versus 7/10 for the baseline;
first-pass completion is 9/10 versus 5/10 (Table~\ref{tab:quality}). The paired
eventual outcomes contain three compositional-only successes and no reverse
discordances; the one-sided exact paired test gives $p=0.125$. For first-pass
completion there are five compositional-only and one baseline-only successes
($p=0.1094$). These small samples support a descriptive improvement, not a
statistically established general advantage. Median generation is
approximately 1.8--1.9 times faster for the single-pass method, which uses
about half as many tokens.

\subsection{Quality Proxies, Validation, and Repair}
The six frozen quality measures show a mixed profile. Compositional outputs
have higher mean merchant-inventory validity, premise-item recall, role
uniqueness, room-canon adherence, and wardrobe-canon term coverage. The
single-pass baseline has higher premise-agent recall (0.5593 versus 0.4632).
The wardrobe proxy favors composition in nine of ten worlds (one-sided sign
test $p=0.0107$, unadjusted across the six measures). These are structural or
lexical proxies; a higher score does not establish aesthetic preference or
better play. We show all six in Figure~\ref{fig:quality-results}, including
the unfavorable comparison.

The seeded-fault suite mutates copies of five completed artifacts and invokes
the production \emph{deterministic validators}, without language-model or
image-generation calls. It detects and attributes all 58 injected faults,
with no flags in 58 corresponding clean checks. Faults cover missing semantic
records, inventories, provenance mismatches, and image/assembly contracts.
The clean checks reuse artifacts across categories, so they are not 58
independent worlds. This result concerns the specified fault suite; it is not
58 successful aesthetic judgments or evidence of universal defect detection.

For five injected role--inventory inconsistencies, localized refinement
restores all five cases while preserving five unaffected intermediate stages.
Mean model-call and token reductions relative to the full-rebuild reference
are 48.0\% and 39.9\%. These observations support repair locality on the seeded
failure type. A wider sample of naturally occurring defects is needed to
estimate its general benefit.

\subsection{Visual and Human-Evaluation Evidence}
The five demonstration worlds connect generated maps, characters, objects,
and persistent interaction. The Tidal Embassy trace includes rejected room
images, local refinement, directional character atlases, and a 25-character
interactive trial with movement, messaging, item use, trade, and a
model-mediated reply (Appendix~\ref{app:tidal}). This is implementation and
case evidence, not a blinded comparison of visual quality.

The completed visual questionnaire evaluates preserved maps, character
samples, and standardized world-structure diagrams. Section~\ref{sec:human-results}
reports the returned judgments separately from runtime reliability and play
experience. The paired structure results do not show a preference advantage
for the full system.

\section{Interaction Across Five Generated Worlds}
\label{sec:interaction}

\subsection{Protocol and Metric Definitions}
The five held-out worlds are Aurora Court of Migrating Cities, Clockwork Rain
Conservatory, Mycelium Patent Bazaar, Sunken Satellite Monastery, and Tidal
Embassy of Lost Languages. Each contains 24--25 main characters and 11--12
discoverable actions spanning five interaction families. At least five
activities per world derive from its premise.

Each accepted run uses Gemini 2.5 Flash for two rounds, activation probability
0.12, and a fixed world-specific seed (26080411--26080415). Agent-profile
generation is not repeated during these runs. Image and video generation are
disabled, and all five runs pass the world-identity audit. The runtime settings
are shared, but the seeds and world contents differ: this is a descriptive
multiworld study, not a paired intervention isolating premise effects.

An \emph{event} is a recorded interaction. An observed participant is any
actor or target ID, including the human interactor where present. A dyad is an
unordered pair of distinct actor and target IDs. If $n_{ij}$ is its event
count, the repeat fraction is
\begin{equation}
\rho=\frac{\sum_{\{i,j\}}\max(n_{ij}-1,0)}{N_{\mathrm{events}}}.
\end{equation}
Action entropy is $H=-\sum_a p(a)\log_2p(a)$ over logged route identifiers.
An open event has a \texttt{proposed\_} route; an explicit world-action event
has a \texttt{world\_action\_} route. Other routes can still encode
premise-specific activities. Thus these categories identify execution
pathways, not an exhaustive semantic classification of world relevance.

Action-result success is computed separately from round summaries:
\begin{equation}
s=\frac{\sum_{w,t}n^{\mathrm{success}}_{w,t}}
{\sum_{w,t}n^{\mathrm{results}}_{w,t}}=\frac{49}{56}=87.5\%.
\end{equation}
An event can include multiple effects or attempts. Neither 53 events nor
37 dyads is the denominator of this rate. Appendix~\ref{app:repro} records the
per-world numerators and denominators.

\subsection{Action Pathways and Consequences}
The runs produce 53 events, 37 within-world unique dyads, ten approved open
proposals, and nine explicit world-action events (Table~\ref{tab:multiworld}).
Sunken Monastery uses eight explicit world actions, including purging corrupt
packets and negotiating bandwidth tithes. Clockwork instead produces seven
open-proposal events out of twelve. This contrast shows two available
execution pathways in use, without proving that one causes better behavior.

In Clockwork, a jammed weather-sheet press becomes a local investigation.
Marius confronts Valerius about the failure; Seraphina asks about etching;
Valerius discusses sheet authenticity. In round two, the same participants
revisit sabotage, inspect a brass folio, and redirect suspicion. Six actions
form this three-character sequence; a seventh open proposal concerns the
market impact of the jam. The approved effects update directed relationships
while the press failure remains part of shared context. The complete episode
diagram is retained in Appendix~\ref{app:cases}.

The resulting state changes differ across worlds. Clockwork accumulates $+3$
net trust and $+21$ influence/fear, whereas Aurora and Tidal accumulate $+70$
and $+88$ trust with zero net influence/fear. These are simulated state
variables produced through the model-mediated interaction machinery. They
show that the traces have persistent consequences, but they are not
independent psychological measurements or evidence that the premise alone
caused the differences.

\subsection{Human Reachability and Failed Attempts}
Thirteen events target the human interactor across four worlds; Clockwork
has none. The logs establish that agents can select the player as a partner
within the shared world. They do not establish how often a human replied,
whether cooperation succeeded, or whether participants enjoyed the encounters.
Those questions require participant-level outcome measures.

Seven of the 56 adjudicated action results are unsuccessful. Retaining these
attempts in the history permits later revision, but this study does not measure
whether failure feedback improves subsequent success. The evidence supports
the availability of an outcome-sensitive interaction loop, consistent with
the broader importance of environment feedback in language agents
\cite{react,reflexion,voyager}.

\section{Historical Context}
Three earlier runs add 1,052 events across an adventuring guild, a black-market
auction, and a storm-struck cruise. Between 23.4\% and 36.5\% of events revisit
an existing dyad, while the largest connected components contain only 11--13
participants. These records motivate studying communication across local
clusters, but configurations differ and spatial scheduling can produce the
observed structure. Full tables and trajectories appear in
Appendix~\ref{sec:pilot}; they are not pooled with the later scripted harness.
