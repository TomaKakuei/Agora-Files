\section{Human Model Comparisons Within Agora}
\label{sec:human-results}

\subsection{Comparison Target and Included Responses}
The human model comparison holds the Agora pipeline and promise-city premise
fixed while varying the recorded language-model configuration. Participants
see a preserved map and the same sampling of two character images per side,
under masked A/B labels. The image generator is shared. Thus the measured
objects are the resulting visual world artifacts under each configuration,
not stand-alone LLM image generation and not competing world-authoring systems.

Ten of eleven returned English visual packets meet the released eligibility
rule. V04 contains all eighteen choices but lacks cover confirmations and
remains pending. The ten eligible returns supply forty image comparisons,
covering all 21 pairs among seven configurations, and 120 item responses.
Four pairs have one rater, sixteen have two, and one has four. Each comparison
asks which world the respondent would rather explore, which map is easier
to read, and which characters better fit their world. These criteria remain
separate; they are not components of a validated overall-quality scale.
The protocol and complete pairwise counts appear in Supplementary Section~\ref{supp-app:human}.

\subsection{Observed Votes by Model Configuration}
Table~\ref{tab:human-model-summary} adds a descriptive view of the model
comparisons. For each configuration and question, we pool its observed wins
and half-weighted ties and divide by its judgeable comparisons. Not sure
choices are excluded from that denominator and remain in the full counts.
This roll-up was added after responses were available to make the pairwise
results readable; it is not a preregistered ranking. Opponent weights differ
because the allocation and one cover exclusion leave unequal pair coverage.

Gemini 2.5 Flash receives eight of ten exploration preferences (80.0\%)
and seven of nine judgeable character-fit preferences (77.8\%). GPT-5.6
Terra receives nine of thirteen exploration preferences (69.2\%) and eleven
of thirteen map-readability preferences (84.6\%); GPT-5.6 Sol receives nine
of eleven readability preferences (81.8\%). The contrast within Sol is
substantial: only four of eleven comparisons favor it for exploration.
Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite receives six of ten exploration preferences but no
readability wins and one readability tie (0.5/10). These are observations
about fixed artifacts, not statistically established model superiority.

\subsection{Criterion-Specific Pairwise Outcomes}
Figure~\ref{fig:human-images} reports all exact model pairs for exploration,
map readability, and character--world fit. Each model name denotes the
recorded world-generation configuration; the pipeline uses a shared image
generator, so the comparison does not isolate an LLM's ability to render
pixels. Only one prompt has matched archived images.

The three criteria give different outcomes. Both raters prefer the GPT-5.6
Sol artifact to Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite for map readability, but both prefer
the latter for exploration and character fit. The pairwise matrix preserves
these distinctions instead of collapsing them into a single model ranking.
Across the image arm there is one readability tie, one character-fit tie,
and two character-fit abstentions. Four pairs now have one rater, sixteen
have two, and one has four. This sparse, single-scene sample supports
inspection of these particular outputs, not a stable ordering of model
families or general claims about visual quality.

\paragraph{Separate internal control and unmeasured structure quality.}
The questionnaire also collected twenty comparisons between two generation
procedures inside Agora. Their full results remain in
Supplementary Section~\ref{supp-app:internal-generation}; the observed diagram preferences favor
the single-pass procedure in aggregate. Both conditions share Agora's
representation, so these controls do not compare Agora with an independent
system. They also do not measure structural differences \emph{between the
seven model configurations}. Human model comparisons here cover maps and
characters; cross-model world-structure preference and actual play experience
were not measured.
