\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Connecting authoring constraints to runtime consequences.}
\agora's mechanism uses the same generated entity identities and relations
through world assembly, action grounding, and state updates. A possession
created during authoring becomes an ownership constraint at execution time;
a committed transfer changes what later actors observe. Dependency-directed
repair maintains the corresponding construction contracts. Language-driven
game construction \cite{word2world,dreamgarden}, shared human--agent play
\cite{light}, and Game Master adjudication \cite{vezhnevets2023} provide direct
precedents. Our contribution is the coupling of these stages through a
premise-conditioned representation and inspectable state transitions.
The construction and execution records show how that coupling operates.

\paragraph{Discoverability and expressiveness are separate design needs.}
A generated repertoire explains what inhabitants can do in a new setting.
Open proposals let a particular encounter depart from that vocabulary.
The Sunken and Clockwork traces illustrate both pathways. A proposal specifies
contextual intent through bounded effects, and the coordinator checks those
effects against shared state. The transferred-item example exposes the full
path from intent to a later inventory observation. Supplementary diagnostics
retain the counts under restricted action and history inputs; differences
in available handlers must be considered when interpreting those counts.
Useful human collaboration requires evidence from actual play.

\paragraph{Internal procedure controls have limited scope.}
Ten shared briefs compare staged and single-pass generation under the same
representation, with different total computation and retry opportunities.
The observed completion gains characterize that implementation comparison,
not the value of Agora's shared representation or its superiority to an
external system. The supplementary diagram judgments favor single-pass
outputs in aggregate and remain reported in full. Both favorable and
unfavorable control outcomes have this same restricted scope.

\paragraph{The visual study is narrow and exploratory.}
Ten eligible coded returns supply forty model-image comparisons; a separate
arm supplies twenty internal structure controls. One of eleven returns lacks
cover confirmations. The image arm covers
one scene, with one to four ratings per exact pair, and the structure arm
shows containment diagrams rather than a complete interactive world. The
source identities are masked, but the artifacts share a generation pipeline
and retain unequal compute. The organizer reports unpaid online volunteer recruitment; an
ethics approval or exemption record and completion times remain undocumented. Accordingly, the findings characterize these
returned artifact judgments, not a stable model ranking or human enjoyment.

\paragraph{Execution is not behavioral validation.}
The five locally observed runs last only two rounds with one seed each. The
21 longer runs use centralized block planning, scripted human events, and each
model's own generated world. The additional planner ablations control the
initial world and model but retain centralized decisions and scripted
interventions. Their high execution success does not validate
emergent coordination; identical relationship increments expose a contribution
from default state-update rules. Historical worlds
are confounded by prior configurations. Relation deltas are model-mediated
state variables, and route entropy depends on the action vocabulary.
Neither measures human trust, fear, affection, or social intelligence directly.
LLM judging also carries systematic biases \cite{judge}; behavioral fidelity
would require independent human data and explicit validation targets, as in
human-grounded agent studies \cite{park1000}.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and human participation.}
Hosted models may change despite a recorded model name; available settings,
seeds, exclusions, and frozen evidence are documented in Supplementary Section~\ref{supp-app:repro}.
The present human study is complete at its reported scope. Evaluating refusal,
outcome legibility, and collaboration during play is a separate future study.
The prospective local-memory protocol in Supplementary Section~\ref{supp-app:intervention}
is also future work, not evidence used for the paper's conclusions. Fictional
roles do not validate demographic models.

\section{Conclusion}
\agora connects a short premise to a persistent social world through three
mechanisms: a shared representation with dependency-directed repair, a
situated interface combining generated actions and open proposals, and
coordinator-governed state transitions. Generated possessions, locations,
and identities become constraints on action; approved effects alter the
state available to subsequent decisions. Construction checks, action traces,
and deterministic replay make these connections inspectable. The completed
visual study characterizes the rendered artifacts, while supplementary
diagnostics document the tested implementation's scope. This provides a
concrete foundation for studying consequential human--AI interaction in
generated settings. Collaboration during play and decentralized social
behavior remain directions for subsequent evaluation.
