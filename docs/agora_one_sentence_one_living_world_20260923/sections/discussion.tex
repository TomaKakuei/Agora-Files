\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{A contribution at the connection between authoring and simulation.}
\agora connects staged world authoring, explicit persistent state, and
grounded social action. Its closest predecessors already establish
language-driven game construction \cite{word2world,dreamgarden}, shared
human--agent play \cite{light}, and Game Master adjudication
\cite{vezhnevets2023}. The contribution is their integration around a
premise-conditioned world representation and inspectable trajectories.
The model comparison varies language-model configurations within Agora. The
generation controls vary its internal authoring procedure. Neither compares
Agora against those independent systems; no cross-system superiority is
established.

\paragraph{Discoverability and expressiveness are separate design needs.}
A generated repertoire explains what inhabitants can do in a new setting.
Open proposals let a particular encounter depart from that vocabulary.
The Sunken and Clockwork traces illustrate both pathways. Their presence
does not establish the benefit of openness: a matched proposal ablation,
with equivalent world and agent states, is needed to estimate that effect.
Likewise, interface access does not establish useful or enjoyable human
collaboration.

\paragraph{Internal procedure controls have limited scope.}
Ten shared briefs compare staged and single-pass generation under the same
representation, with different total computation and retry opportunities.
The observed completion gains characterize that implementation comparison,
not the value of Agora's shared representation or its superiority to an
external system. The supplementary diagram judgments favor single-pass
outputs in aggregate and remain reported in full. Both favorable and
unfavorable control outcomes have this same restricted scope.

\paragraph{The visual study is narrow and exploratory.}
Ten eligible coded returns supply forty model-image comparisons; a separate
arm supplies twenty internal structure controls. One of eleven returns lacks
cover confirmations. The image arm covers
one scene, with one to four ratings per exact pair, and the structure arm
shows containment diagrams rather than a complete interactive world. The
source identities are masked, but the artifacts share a generation pipeline
and retain unequal compute. The organizer reports unpaid online volunteer recruitment; an
ethics approval or exemption record and completion times remain undocumented. Accordingly, the findings characterize these
returned artifact judgments, not a stable model ranking or human enjoyment.

\paragraph{Execution is not behavioral validation.}
The five locally observed runs last only two rounds with one seed each. The
21 longer runs use centralized block planning, scripted human events, and each
model's own generated world. Their high execution success does not validate
emergent coordination; identical relationship increments expose a contribution
from default state-update rules. Historical worlds
are confounded by prior configurations. Relation deltas are model-mediated
state variables, and route entropy depends on the action vocabulary.
Neither measures human trust, fear, affection, or social intelligence directly.
LLM judging also carries systematic biases \cite{judge}; behavioral fidelity
would require independent human data and explicit validation targets, as in
human-grounded agent studies \cite{park1000}.

\paragraph{A prospective intervention is not a completed result.}
The Tidal protocol varies access to prior social episodes while preserving
current world state. No completed paired 32-round results or blinded annotations
are reported in this release. The available record contains a local protocol,
but no external registration identifier; we therefore describe it as a
prospective design. Three seed pairs would provide a feasibility estimate with
substantial uncertainty, not precise population inference.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and human participation.}
Hosted models may change despite a recorded model name; available settings,
seeds, exclusions, and frozen evidence are documented in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.
Human studies should evaluate refusal, outcome legibility, and collaboration
under unequal information. Fictional roles do not validate demographic models.

\section{Conclusion}
\agora turns a short premise into a rendered social environment whose
inhabitants can act on persistent shared state. The completed studies show
criterion-specific visual preferences across seven model configurations,
a reliability--cost tradeoff in an internal generation control, use of
explicit world actions and open proposals, and a gap between successful
execution and material consequences
in a separate 21-trajectory audit.
The central result is an inspectable connection between world authoring and
consequential interaction. Broader human evaluation and controlled
interventions are needed to determine how well the generated worlds support
collaboration and which mechanisms shape their longer social trajectories.
