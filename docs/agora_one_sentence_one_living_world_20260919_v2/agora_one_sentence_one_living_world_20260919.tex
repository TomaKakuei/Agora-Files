\noindent\textbf{Abstract.}
Generating a social world requires more than a coherent scene: inhabitants
must be able to change shared spatial, material, and social state. We present
\agora, a system that compiles a one-sentence premise into a persistent
rendered environment with rooms, roles, institutions, possessions, private
knowledge, and visual identity. Humans and language agents use a generated
action repertoire or propose context-specific actions; a World Coordinator
evaluates their effects before updating shared state. Across ten paired
world briefs, compositional generation completes 10/10 worlds versus 7/10 for a
single-pass baseline, at higher token and latency cost. Five two-round
interaction runs produce 53 events, ten approved open proposals, and thirteen
events targeting the human interactor; 49/56 adjudicated action results
succeed. A separate retrospective audit covers 24 one-sentence generation trials and
21 scripted 24-round trajectories: 1,365/1,512 model actions execute
successfully, yet only 398 successes change inventory or location. The longer
harness uses centralized planning, and its relation updates mostly repeat a
fixed rule. These observations distinguish construction and execution
reliability from behavioral fidelity or causal social effects. We release
reproducible figures, bilingual artifact-evaluation materials, and a
prospective memory-intervention protocol. \agora connects language-based world
authoring to inspectable trajectories of what inhabitants do together.
